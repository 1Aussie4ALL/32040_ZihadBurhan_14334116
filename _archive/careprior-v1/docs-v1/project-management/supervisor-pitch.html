CarePrior

AI-assisted intake. Human-led decisions.

An explainable, human-supervised AI clinical intake and prioritisation prototype for primary healthcare — and, as of this week, a working local build, not just a proposal.

Candidate: Zihad Burhan (14334116) Program: Master of IT, UTS Prototype running locally · core case study verified end-to-end
01

The problem

Primary-care and telehealth intake is a triage bottleneck staffed by people, not clinicians — and today's tooling doesn't help them.

VagueFree-text descriptions with no structure ("tired and a bit breathless")
FixedStatic forms ask every patient the same questions, regardless of situation
HiddenWarning signs buried in ordinary language, reviewed by non-clinical staff under time pressure

The research question: can a hybrid AI + deterministic-rule system, with adaptive questioning and mandatory human review, identify requests needing earlier attention more consistently than a static form — without ever pretending to diagnose?

02

The solution, at a glance

Every request passes through the same explainable pipeline before a human ever sees it.

03

What could go wrong — and how the design answers it

This is the part most "AI triage" pitches skip. It's the part the design was built around.

The concern

AI hallucinates or misses a genuinely urgent case (false negative).

How CarePrior responds

Deterministic rules run independently of the AI and can never be downgraded by it. Urgent-case recall / false-negative rate is the headline evaluation metric, not accuracy.

The concern

Staff stop thinking critically and just click "accept" (automation bias).

How CarePrior responds

Human approval is mandatory on every request. Overrides require a written reason. Patients never see AI confidence. Override rate and direction are tracked as a metric.

The concern

Patient information is incomplete, vague, or self-contradictory.

How CarePrior responds

Adaptive questioning targets exactly the missing fields. Contradictions route to "Manual Review Required" instead of being silently guessed at.

The concern

"Black box" AI triage staff can't interrogate or trust.

How CarePrior responds

Every recommendation ships with a plain-language reasoning summary, the exact patient answers that triggered it, and the specific rule name — visible before any decision is made.

The concern

No accountability trail if something goes wrong.

How CarePrior responds

Append-only audit log across consent, AI output, rule triggers, recommendations and staff decisions. Nothing can be edited or deleted through the application.

The concern

Reads as "just another symptom-checker chatbot."

How CarePrior responds

Built as an enterprise-style platform: 6 RBAC roles, versioned deterministic rules, a 4-mode research-evaluation harness — positioned and engineered as clinical decision support, not a chatbot.

The concern

Scope is too large for one solo semester build.

How CarePrior responds

Delivered as a working vertical slice first — the full case-study scenario end-to-end — with breadth added in phases on top of a proven skeleton, not attempted all at once.

05

The 3-minute version of the demo

Full script runs 7–10 minutes; this is what I'd show first if time is short.

  1. Patient logs in, gives consent, describes symptoms vaguely: "tired and slightly short of breath."
  2. System asks adaptive follow-up questions — patient discloses chest pressure, sweating, arm discomfort.
  3. Submits — the deterministic cardiac-warning rule fires alongside AI interpretation.
  4. Staff dashboard shows the request at the top of the queue as Immediate Human Review, with full reasoning and the exact matched answers.
  5. Reviewer accepts or overrides (reason required) — then opens the audit explorer to show the untouchable end-to-end trail.
06

What this is actually testing

The platform is the experimental apparatus, not just the deliverable.

RQ1Can a hybrid AI + rule system flag requests for earlier review more effectively than a static form?
RQ2Does adaptive questioning measurably improve prioritisation over fixed-field intake?
RQ3Do explanations improve staff understanding of, and trust in, a recommendation?
RQ4How should the system behave when information is incomplete, ambiguous or contradictory?
RQ5How do we reduce the risk of automation bias and staff overreliance?
RQ6How does hybrid compare against static-form / rule-only / AI-only modes on the same scenarios?
RQ7What privacy, security and audit controls does AI-assisted intake actually require?
