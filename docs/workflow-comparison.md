# Workflow Comparison: Conventional Booking vs. This System

Work package **6.4**. The measured results are in [`evaluation-results.md`](evaluation-results.md),
regenerated by `npm run evaluate`. This document explains *what changes about the workflow itself*
— who does what, when, and with what information in front of them.

## 1. The two workflows, step by step

### Conventional online GP booking

| # | Step | Who | Information available |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Choose a doctor and an available time | Patient | Doctor names, specialties, open slots |
| 2 | Type a reason for the visit | Patient | One free-text field, typically 1–2 sentences |
| 3 | Submit; the request joins the queue | System | Name, contact details, chosen slot, free text |
| 4 | Reception may skim the reason | Reception | The same free text, read under time pressure |
| 5 | Consultation begins; history-taking starts from zero | GP | Whatever the patient wrote, plus prior records |

The urgency of the appointment is decided by **which slot happened to be free** and **when the
patient chose to book**. Nothing in the process ranks one request against another.

### This system

| # | Step | Who | Information available |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enter contact details | Patient | — |
| 2 | Choose a complaint category and describe the concern | Patient | Free text, same as conventional |
| 3 | Answer adaptive structured questions | Patient | 7 answers on average; follow-ups unlock from earlier answers |
| 4 | Deterministic safety rules run | System | 19 rules over explicit, patient-confirmed answers |
| 5 | AI assessment runs and is schema-validated | System | Structured answers only — never raw free text alone |
| 6 | Urgency assigned; AI may raise it above the safety floor but never lower it | System | Safety floor + validated AI output |
| 7 | Patient sees the urgency, the reasoning, and timing guidance | Patient | Plain-language explanation, emergency advice where relevant |
| 8 | Request enters a queue **ordered by urgency** | System | Full structured record |
| 9 | Clinician reviews every request before it is final | Clinician | Pre-consultation summary, triggered rules, missing-information list, audit trail |

## 2. What actually changes

### The queue gains an ordering

This is the structural difference. A conventional queue is chronological: a patient with chest
tightness who books on Thursday sits behind a routine check-up booked on Tuesday. Here the queue
is sorted by urgency, with the clinical override preserved above the system's own assessment
(`getClinicianQueue` in `src/lib/database/requests.ts`).

### Work moves from the consultation to before it

The structured questions take the patient a few minutes at booking time. In exchange, the
clinician opens the request already knowing duration, severity, progression, functional impact,
associated symptoms and the category-specific red-flag answers — an average of **8.0 clinical data
points versus 1.0**, measured across all 26 scenarios.

### "I don't know" becomes an explicit outcome

A conventional form cannot distinguish an ordinary request from one it has no information about;
both look like a booking. This system has a fifth urgency level, `InsufficientInformation`, and
all four vague scenarios land there rather than being silently booked as routine.

### The failure mode changes shape

Conventional booking fails *silently and invisibly* — nobody knows a request was mis-prioritised
until the consultation, or later. This system fails *loudly*: a request it cannot assess is
flagged, every request carries its reasoning, and every one is queued for human review.

## 3. Measured outcome

From [`evaluation-results.md`](evaluation-results.md), all three workflows scored over the same
26 scenarios:

| Workflow | Accuracy | Under-triage | Emergency recall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conventional — no triage | 19.2% | 65.4% | 0.0% |
| Conventional — keyword triage | 50.0% | 30.8% | 83.3% |
| This system | 100.0% | 0.0% | 100.0% |

The single most informative case is `SCN-EDGE02`. The patient writes *"Feeling pretty flat lately,
nothing major"* and rates their severity 2/10 — but answers the structured mental-health safety
question to say they have frequent thoughts of self-harm **with a plan**. No keyword scan of that
free text can find the risk, because the risk is not in the text. The structured question is the
only thing that surfaces it.

The inverse case, `SCN-R05`, is equally instructive: *"repeat prescription for my regular blood
pressure medication"* trips a keyword scan on the word *blood* and gets escalated. Word matching
produces both misses and false alarms, and a structured question ("what do you need?" → repeat
prescription) produces neither.

## 4. What this comparison does *not* show

Stated plainly, because the table above is easy to over-read:

- **The system's 100% is self-consistency, not clinical validity.** The 26 scenarios were written
  to exercise this system's own logic paths, and the expected urgencies are the author's labels,
  not a clinical gold standard. A scenario set written by someone else would score differently.
- **The keyword baseline is a model, not a measurement of real reception staff.** Its score depends
  entirely on how the scenario free text happens to be worded, and those texts were written
  alongside the system. Real reception staff also have a telephone, prior records, and the ability
  to ask a follow-up question — none of which the baseline gets.
- **Patient effort is not measured.** This workflow asks the patient to answer around seven
  questions where a conventional form asks for one sentence. Whether patients tolerate that, and
  whether they answer honestly, is exactly the kind of question a real user study would need to
  answer. See the usability limitations in [`evaluation-results.md`](evaluation-results.md).
- **No real patients, no real outcomes, no clinician time measured.** The claim "work moves from
  the consultation to before it" is a design intent supported by the data-point count, not a
  measured reduction in consultation time.

See [`limitations.md`](limitations.md) for the full list.
